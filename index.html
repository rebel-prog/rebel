REBEL

Rethinking Exclusions and Biased Evidence in Linguistics

A two-day workshop in Brno, Czech Republic — 27–28 September 2027.

What counts as natural language, and who counts as a speaker? REBEL brings together critiques of how linguistics — as an institutionalised practice — draws the boundaries of its field, and who gets left outside them.

Read the call for papers Registration

About the workshop

What belongs to natural language grammar, and what does not, has never been entirely self-evident. The boundaries of linguistics have been contested, negotiated, and reshaped over time — shaped by disciplinary conventions, institutional structures, social hierarchies, and power relations.

These inclusions and exclusions also decide what we accept as evidence: which observations are treated as direct evidence about grammar, and which are set aside as noise, performance effects, confounds, or individual variation. The first REBEL workshop brings together different strands of criticism of how this line gets drawn.

Who the workshop is for

REBEL welcomes linguists working across any language, modality, population, or type of data who want to examine the assumptions, categories, and boundaries underlying linguistic research. We especially encourage early-career researchers, including advanced BA/MA students and PhD candidates.
